Two conclusions that change the plan:
1. **Collective pitch barely moves the blade-root fatigue DEL on the 5 MW** (±3 %; the baseline paper found 0.2 % on the 15 MW). It moves **tower-base fore-aft DEL by 15–30 %** as a by-product of smoother speed regulation. With the blade-only fitness, genuinely useful policies score ≈ 0 or negative.
2. **The R2 agent's premise (positive pitch residual reduces blade fatigue) does not hold in OpenFAST**: at U8 it only loses energy; at U12.5 it raises blade DEL. The value of region specialisation is in R3 (and in *not* letting one policy trade R3 regulation for R2 load), which is exactly what the toy showed for mono.

## 3. Decisions the user has to make (when back)

1. **Load objective**: keep blade-root DEL (decision Q5) knowing collective pitch cannot move it much, switch to tower-base fore-aft DEL, or a weighted combination. My recommendation after E3: tower (or combined), because it is the quantity the residual demonstrably controls and it is what the baseline paper reports too.
2. **Speed-std constraint tolerance**: strict (≤ GSPI) makes any residual activity in R3 look like failure on OpenFAST; a 2 % tolerance (`F_tol2`, already reported alongside F) or a *speed-MSE* criterion like the paper might be more informative.
3. **R2 role**: keep the R2 agent (pitch-only, non-negative residual) or freeze it (R3 specialist + oracle router) until torque residuals (ROSCO patch) give R2 a real objective.
4. **Guardrail for OpenFAST**: rollback-to-ep-0 during the first 100 episodes prevents any learning when improvements are slow; proposal: `--rollback_after 120` and rollback only on constraint violation, not on F noise.

## 7. LLM supervisor v2 (2026-08-30 afternoon) — literature scan and design

Recent work adapted (2025–2026):
- **RHyVE** (arXiv 2604.28056): LLM-generated rewards are *hypotheses*; verify them by **short-horizon forks from a shared
  checkpoint** and deploy phase-aware (verification is uninformative while the policy is incompetent).
- **LLMZero** (2606.18388): LLM agents diagnose training checkpoints and adjust parameters; discovered strategies show
  "capacity parameters monotone, regularisation parameters oscillating" — the λ curriculum we observed.
- **LERO** (2503.21807) / **Agent²** (2509.13368) / **AlphaEvolve-style** program evolution (TurboEvolve 2604.18607,
  optimize_anything 2605.19633): population + archive of candidates, top performers seed the next LLM generation;
  *actionable side-information beats score-only feedback*.
- **ControlAgent** (2410.19811): LLM control design with iterative verification against specs.

What was built (`--supervisor llm_fork | random_fork | schedule`):
1. The LLM proposes **K=3 deliberately different candidate knob vectors** (conservative / aggressive / structural).
2. Each candidate is **fork-trained one wave from the same checkpoint and evaluated on 2 wind seeds**; the best fork
   (tier first, then F) is kept — LLM-guided population-based training. All fork episodes count toward the budget.
3. The LLM sees every past candidate's outcome (archive) and the tier-aware guardrail only rolls back on constraint violation.
4. Controls: `random_fork` (same forks, random candidates → isolates the LLM), `schedule` (replay of the E5 LLM's
   knob trajectory → isolates the curriculum), plus P2 (single-proposal LLM with 2-seed evaluation).

Queued as P5 after the P-campaign; results go into section 8.

Answer (single RL seed, one held-out wind seed — to be replicated):
- **Yes, when its proposals are verified.** Fork-verified LLM supervision is the only configuration that beats the
  fixed-weight baseline on unseen wind *while staying strictly non-inferior to ROSCO*: +20.9 vs +16.1 (guard) and
  +16.2 (random candidates with the identical fork machinery). The fork machinery itself adds nothing with random
  candidates (16.2 ≈ 16.1); the gain comes from the candidate quality, i.e. the LLM's reading of the diagnostics.
- **How**: every one of its five decisions was a strict fork; it alternated conservative and aggressive candidates and
  the fork test picked the aggressive one exactly when the policy was ready (ep 144, 256) and rejected it otherwise
  (ep 32, 88, 200) — competence-aware deployment in the sense of RHyVE. Its final knobs (λ_R3 3.8→6, Δβ_R3 0.02,

  unseen wind; (ii) replaying the curriculum as a fixed schedule: same tower reduction (−19.9 %) but the speed
  constraint slips (tol2) — the timing relative to the policy's state matters, not just the trajectory.

## 15. R2 torque residual (2026-09-02 → 09-03, mac) — implementation, three false trails, one real bug, and a clear negative result

Implementation (all backward compatible, `--dtau_max` flag): Controllers.f90 patch applies
`ZMQ_TorqueOffset` at the avrSWAP(47) write (after the PI state reset — native controller state
never sees the residual); 2nd action dim (R2-only, ±dtau_max, own 2nd-order damper, clamped to
[0, VS_MaxTq]); act_dim plumbed through PPO/rollout/evaluate.

**Debugging chronicle (kept honest — the failure analyses up to the last one were symptom-chasing):**
1. tq_on_s0 (2000 Nm): catastrophic (spd ratio to 6.5, Eloss 7–10%, rollback-to-ep0 every wave);
   toy sweep failed even at 250 Nm ⇒ not exploration scale.
2. WSE hypothesis — real correctness issue, fixed (Filters.f90/ControllerBlocks.f90: the wind-speed
   estimator now consumes native+offset; verified v_est error unchanged at ±2000 Nm) — but tq_on2k_s0
   still failed identically.
3. Overspeed-exploit hypothesis (constant-torque P = τω grows past rated while the oracle label can
   stay R2; label = reward attack surface) — mathematically real loophole; speed gate (residual only
   below 0.98×rated ⇒ channel is a deep-R2 instrument, inert at 12.5 m/s) + rated-power cap added —
   tq_on2kg_s0 still failed.
4. Kinetic-draining hypothesis (rotor KE timescale 20–60 s >> γ=0.998's 5 s horizon) — also a real
   loophole (probe: const +2000 Nm@8 m/s ⇒ P +3% short-window while the rotor drains); KE-exact
   accounting added to the R2 task term (credit P + η·d(½Jω²)/dt, EMA 1 s, active only with the
   channel on; verified: the draining strategy now scores r_task −9.0 vs −0.3) — tq_on2kk_s0 STILL failed.
5. **Actual root cause: `rollout.py` packed actions as `reshape(-1,1)` — with act_dim=2 the (T,2)
   array became (2T,1): actions misaligned with obs/log-probs, every PPO update garbage, policies
   railed to ±1 within one wave.** All four OpenFAST failures and the toy sweep were this bug.
   Fixed (`reshape(len(A),-1)`); toy retest healthy; pitch-only path bit-identical (regression
   F 4.20/12.17 reproduced). The three guards are kept (WSE = correctness; gate/cap/KE = principled
   protections against real loopholes) but their individual necessity is untested.

**Result (tq_on3, all guards + fix, dtau 2000 Nm, 5 seeds, seed-paired vs tq_off control, spec+guard,
tower objective, held-out S3–S6 best ckpt):**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| tq_off (pitch only) | 11.8, 16.0, 13.4, 16.7, 11.9 | **13.9 ± 2.3** | 5/5 | 0.26 % |

## 16. LPV-MPC baseline (2026-09-03 afternoon/evening) — baselines are now GSPI + MPC

**Final controller** (after three diagnosed failure modes): 3-state LPV-MPC — rotor + first tower
fore-aft mode (0.324 Hz, modal mass 437 t), linearised every Ts = 0.1 s at the current operating

1. 1-state rotor model: excellent on the 1-DOF toy (speed std 0.03 vs GSPI 0.48) but pumps the

2. 3-state model still hunted at ~0.15 Hz; speed filtering made it worse (lag). Discriminating

3. Tower-velocity cost term (qt > 0) at any tested weight drives the optimum to feather
   (Eloss 71 %): a pure xd² term prefers zero thrust; a proper multi-objective MPC would need a
   power-tracking term — left as a limitation, qt = 0.

   MPC override distorts (U15 frac_R3 → 0.4–0.5, speed constraint blind ⇒ "strict" with tower
