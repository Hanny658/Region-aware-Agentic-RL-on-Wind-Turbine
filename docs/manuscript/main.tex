\begin{abstract}
We put a large-language-model agent in the simulation loop of a wind turbine's pitch controller and measure what it delivers, in the four reductions the field publishes, on wind realisations that no part of the design saw. The agent proposes complete tuning files for the gain-scheduled PI controller that ROSCO ships for the NREL~5\,MW turbine; OpenFAST evaluates each file on fourteen above-rated episodes and a constrained rule keeps or discards it. In eight simulations the agent found that changing one line of the shipped file, the corner frequency of the generator-speed filter, which the toolbox sets by a rule of thumb, reduces generator-speed mean-square error by 23.0\,\% and tower-base fatigue by 8.7\,\% at exactly the shipped pitch duty, with both load terms holding at every wind speed on 56 held-out episodes; a second row reaches 28.4\,\% and 16.3\,\% for 1.65 times the travel. Random search over the same box finds nothing in 72 candidates. The same loop, with nothing changed but the object it writes, repairs an untuned offset-free LPV-MPC whose tower-base fatigue at 24\,m/s is 8.8 points worse than the shipped controller's, bringing it inside tolerance in 24 simulations where random search does not in 48, and writes the reward structure of a learned residual layer that adds 5.8 to 6.4 points of power regulation on top of the tuned baseline without losing tower fatigue in any of three training seeds. Across 144 candidates on the plant, none the agent proposed destabilised it, against 13 of 144 for a local search, 9 of 144 for Bayesian optimisation and 16 of 72 for random draws. Every number is measured under one protocol: load constraints required at every mean wind speed and measured off the design winds, paired stratified bootstraps over episodes, and actuator duty beside every row.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Above rated wind speed, collective pitch regulates rotor speed and electrical power while the tower and the blades accumulate fatigue. The controller a turbine actually runs is, in the reference implementations the field shares, a gain-scheduled PI loop on generator speed with a handful of filters and a tower damper~\citep{Bossanyi2000,Abbas2022rosco}, and it ships with a tuning. Parts of that tuning are the result of a design procedure; parts are rules of thumb. The corner frequency of ROSCO's generator-speed filter, for instance, is set to a quarter of the blade's first edgewise frequency~\citep{Abbas2022rosco}, a turbine-agnostic default that no simulation has examined. Every comparison that takes the shipped controller as its baseline starts from wherever those defaults happen to leave it.

Re-tuning such a controller is a search over a handful of physically meaningful parameters, each with a unit and a role the designer can name, against a simulation that returns a few load and regulation statistics per candidate. That is a problem a language model can read and write: the candidate is a tuning file, the feedback is a table, and the constraints are sentences. This paper puts a reasoning language model in exactly that position (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}) and measures what comes out, on three objects of increasing distance from the model's prior knowledge: the shipped pitch controller's own parameters, the cost structure of a model-predictive controller it did not design, and the reward expression of a learned residual layer. In all three the model proposes, the simulator decides, and nothing the model says is kept unless the plant confirms it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An LLM agent in the tuning loop of a wind turbine controller.} To our knowledge this is the first instance in wind turbine control design; the mechanism has precedents in other domains, which §\ref{sec:related} attributes. The agent proposes complete ROSCO tuning files; OpenFAST and a constrained selection rule decide.
\item \textbf{What the shipped controller leaves on the table, in files it can take.} One line of the shipped tuning is worth $+23.0$\,\% of generator-speed regulation and $+8.7$\,\% of tower-base fatigue at the shipped pitch duty; the verified band of agent-found tunings is a duty ladder from 0.97 to 2.02 times the shipped travel, every member holding both load terms at every wind speed on 56 episodes it was not designed on (§\ref{sec:leg1}).
\item \textbf{One loop, three design spaces, no rebuild.} The same agent, with no acquisition function, no population and no per-space surrogate, finds a feasible tuning where random search finds none on a PI controller, an MPC cost structure and a reward grammar (§\ref{sec:leg1}--\ref{sec:leg3}).
\item \textbf{Proposal admissibility.} None of the agent's 144 candidates destabilised the plant, against 13, 9 and 16 of the classical and random arms' candidates; its proposals were well-formed in every one of six runs. The agent spends no simulation budget on controllers that cannot run (§\ref{sec:search}).
\item \textbf{A measurement protocol} under which the above is believable: load constraints held per wind speed and measured off the design winds, paired stratified bootstraps over episodes, one aggregation rule, and pitch travel and duty cycle beside every row (§\ref{sec:setup}, §\ref{sec:discipline}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} One onshore turbine, collective pitch, above rated, one turbulence class, simulation only. The loop is the contribution; the numbers belong to this plant.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Language-model agents that tune controllers.} Agents that design controllers with a language model proposing and a computation agent checking exist as a general construction~\citep{Guo2024controlagent}, and the specific case of a model proposing PID gains against simulation-verified targets has been reported for chemical processes~\citep{Shou2026pidllm}. \citet{Chen2026llmranking} benchmark open-weight models at ranking PI gain sets and find 31.8--53.2\,\% accuracy, concluding that simulator-based validation remains necessary before a model's judgement is used for tuning; that conclusion is the design principle of the loop here, in which the model never ranks and the simulator always does. Reward design by language models runs from program synthesis against a task score~\citep{Ma2024eureka,Yu2023l2r,Xie2024text2reward,Ma2024dreureka,Lee2026rda,Gao2026rfagent} to engineering plants~\citep{Cardenoso2025learnopt,Casas2026large}, including the adaptation of reward \emph{weights} on a real energy system~\citep{Wu2026llmrewardweight}; placing the model inside the fast loop as the controller itself is the opposite design choice~\citep{Rasheed2025llmcontrol}. On the comparison that matters for an agent used as an optimiser, \citet{Li2026greedy} find that greedy iteration is a strong default and that elaborate search adds little at two to three times the evaluations, and \citet{Bigdeli2026falsifiers} report a budget-matched comparison in a cyber-physical setting in which a language model proposes and a simulator and monitor decide. We found no instance of a language model in a wind turbine control-design loop.\footnote{One conference record whose title could contain one, O.~Teke, \emph{The Role of Artificial Intelligence Agents in Optimization and Control of Wind Energy Systems}, 6th International Conference on Engineering, Natural and Social Sciences (ICENSOS 2025), Konya, has no abstract, DOI or full text online (the series' proceedings site carries only its 2023 volume) and could not be checked.}

\paragraph{Tuning the shipped controller is a published problem.} \citet{Zalkind2020tuning} tune this layer automatically by zeroth-order optimisation over aeroelastic simulations and report 20--26\,\% cost reductions over the defaults on the same reference turbine. ROSCO's tuning procedure sets the filter corner by a generic rule~\citep{Abbas2022rosco}, and the controller runs on industrial hardware~\citep{Corredera2026plc}, so the baseline here is the one a turbine would run, not a strawman.

\paragraph{MPC for wind turbine pitch.} Linear-parameter-varying formulations are established~\citep{Soliman2011mpc,Koerber2013mpc,Mulders2020qlpv}, with preview~\citep{Schlipf2013nmpc}, robust~\citep{Evans2015rmpc,Klein2025robustmpc} and field-tested variants whose authors concede that ``the tuning effort still remains complex''~\citep{Klein2025robustmpc,Wintermeyer2023challenges}. Two elements of the controller in §\ref{sec:leg2} are prior art and we claim neither: scheduling the cost weights on an operating variable is due to \citet{Wintermeyer2021weightsched}, and offset-free MPC in wind energy to \citet{Liu2024offsetfree}. \citet{Anand2026adaptive} repair the model of an economic MPC for the same turbine in the same simulator with an offline-trained network; the repair in §\ref{sec:leg2} is of the cost, not the model, and is found online by the agent.

\paragraph{Learned pitch control and residual policies.} Learned collective and individual pitch controllers are reported against a PI or GSPI baseline on a few wind speeds~\citep{SierraGarcia2020,Xie2023rlpitch,Coquelet2022ipc,Baseline2026,Espinoza2026ddqn}, and from-scratch learned controllers start below their baseline until warm-started~\citep{Nilsen2026expert}. Restricting where a residual may act is part of the construction: \citet{Abbas2026specialized,Abbas2023specializedarxiv} activate a residual only in conditions identified by a hidden Markov model, \citet{Kim2026stabilityaware} suppress one inside a tracking-error deadband, \citet{Cao2026autosafe} compose a learned policy with a safety-structured base. The gate in §\ref{sec:leg3} is theirs in mechanism; what is ours is the gating variable, an exogenous wind-speed estimate the base controller already computes, and the measurement.

\paragraph{Evaluation conventions.} Short-term damage-equivalent loads~\citep{IEC61400,fatpack}, site weighting and actuator duty are the field's instruments; \citet{Requate2020evaluation} show that the seed count the standard asks for can be too few, which is why every held-out claim here is made on two disjoint sets of four seeds and reported on both.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Plant, wind protocol, metrics, statistics}
\label{sec:protocol}
The plant is the NREL~5\,MW onshore turbine~\citep{Jonkman2009nrel5mw} in OpenFAST~4.2.1~\citep{OpenFAST}, driven at a 10\,ms control step by ROSCO~2.10.5~\citep{Abbas2022rosco} through a patched ZeroMQ interface. The pairing reference throughout is the shipped ROSCO gain-scheduled PI with peak shaving on, every other load-mitigation feature off and constant generator torque above rated; every number in this paper is a percentage reduction against that controller on the same wind file, and a reduction is written positive.

\paragraph{Wind.} Seven mean hub wind speeds, 12 to 24\,m/s in steps of 2; TurbSim fields of 650\,s with the Kaimal spectrum, a power-law shear profile and turbulence characteristic~B of IEC~61400-1 edition~3~\citep{IEC61400}, from which 600\,s episodes are simulated, the first 20\,s discarded and the remaining 580\,s scored. The tower-base moment's RMS in the first 20\,s is 6.9\,\% above its settled level and 0.9\,\% in the second; lengthening the discard to 100\,s moves the shipped controller's tower fatigue by $-0.5$\,\% and its power MSE by $-4.2$\,\%, shifts of a level that both controllers of a comparison share and that cancel in the ratios reported. Seeds 1--2 are the design winds and the only winds any selection sees (14 episodes); seeds 3--6 are held out (28 episodes); seeds 7--10 (28 episodes) were generated after every controller was frozen (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}).

\paragraph{The four reductions.} Power MSE and generator-speed MSE on above-rated steps labelled by hub wind speed; tower-base fore-aft and blade-root out-of-plane damage-equivalent loads from rainflow counting at W\"ohler exponents 4 and 10, $\big(\sum_i n_i S_i^m/n_{\mathrm{ref}}\big)^{1/m}$~\citep{fatpack,IEC61400}, with $n_{\mathrm{ref}}$ cancelling in every ratio; the energy change over the episode; the pitch travel relative to the shipped controller; and the actuator duty cycle, the mean absolute pitch rate against the 10\,deg/s rate limit. These are the published set of a recent learned pitch-control study~\citep{Baseline2026}. The two regulation terms are not independent above rated, where torque is constant: their reductions correlate at 0.94--0.95 on model-based rows and only 0.19--0.83 on PI rows, so where one of them is used alone as an objective (§\ref{sec:leg1}) the other is reported beside it. The campaigns of §\ref{sec:leg2} and §\ref{sec:leg3} selected and trained on a composite of these terms; that composite is their selection objective, named as such in App.~\ref{app:objective}, and is never a reported number here.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every controller is deterministic given the wind field, so the uncertainty of a fixed controller is over wind realisations: differences between controllers are paired stratified bootstraps over episodes, strata the mean wind speed, quoted as 95\,\% intervals in square brackets. Across training seeds every seed is reported. The held-out evidence is eight seeds per wind speed in two disjoint sets of four, reported separately and never pooled.

\paragraph{Load constraints are per wind speed, measured off the design winds.} A constraint on the set mean of a load term is gamed across wind speeds, with learning and without it: this study found four instances in which a controller holding the set mean lost 2.7 to 8.8 points of a load term at a single wind speed (§\ref{sec:discipline}). The rule under which a tuning is said to \emph{hold} is therefore that neither load term falls more than 1 point below the shipped controller at \emph{any} of the seven mean wind speeds, on both held-out sets. On the 14-episode design set, with two episodes per wind, the same rule is only a screen: it is applied there to choose, and applied on 56 episodes to decide.

\subsection{The loop}
\label{sec:loop}
Figure~\ref{fig:loop} is the whole method. A proposer writes a candidate as a JSON object; the deterministic half of the loop evaluates it on the design set, computes the reductions and the constraints with their bootstrap intervals, appends the result to a history, and the proposer sees the whole history on its next call. The proposer never ranks candidates and never decides what is kept. Four proposers are compared on the same budget of 24 candidates from the same shipped start: independent random draws from the box; a $(1+\lambda)$ local search around the incumbent; constrained Bayesian optimisation with a Gaussian-process surrogate; and the language-model agent, a reasoning model shown the box, the five quantities with their units and physical meaning, the objective, the constraints and the history, in JSON mode at reasoning effort \emph{medium}, one call per candidate, with every reply audited for well-formedness. The model used is \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and the same loop with the same prompt shape, the same history and no other machinery is reused on the two other design spaces of §\ref{sec:leg2} and §\ref{sec:leg3}.

\subsection{The search space on the shipped controller, and the rule}
\label{sec:space}
ROSCO builds its pitch gain schedule from two numbers in the tuning file, the loop's target natural frequency $\omega_{pc}$ and damping ratio $\zeta_{pc}$, as $K_p=\big(2\zeta_{pc}\omega_{pc}+A(\beta)\big)/B(\beta)$ and $K_i=\omega_{pc}^2/B(\beta)$ with $A,B$ aerodynamic sensitivities read from the plant; the shipped file has $\zeta_{pc}=0.7$, $\omega_{pc}=0.6$\,rad/s, and $A,B$ are recovered from the shipped tables with a round-trip error of $8.7\times10^{-19}$. A candidate is therefore five numbers, every one of which the controller can take: $\omega_{pc}$, $\zeta_{pc}$, the generator-speed low-pass corner \texttt{F\_LPFCornerFreq}, and the gain \texttt{FA\_KI} and high-pass corner \texttt{FA\_HPFCornerFreq} of the tower fore-aft damper. Every box limit is measured on the plant rather than chosen: $\omega_{pc}$ runs from a 10\,s loop time constant up to the first tower fore-aft mode, 2.04\,rad/s, which a collective-pitch loop must not drive; $\zeta_{pc}$ from 0.10 to 2.00, nearly undamped to heavily overdamped against the shipped 0.7; the filter corner from 0.20\,rad/s to 19.2\,rad/s, above which a first-order filter passes 99.99\,\% of the measured generator-speed power and is indistinguishable from none; the damper gain from zero to 0.20\,rad$\cdot$s/m, at which its demand reaches 12.3$^\circ$ on a median episode, with its integral limiter fixed at 10$^\circ$ and reported whenever it binds; the damper high-pass from 0.05 to 1.20\,rad/s (App.~\ref{app:search}). The far corner of this box is unstable by construction, and an infeasible region inside the box is evidence that the box is wide enough.

The selection rule maximises the generator-speed regulation reduction subject to tower DEL, blade DEL and energy being no worse than shipped, where a constraint counts as violated only when its loss exceeds its own 95\,\% paired-bootstrap interval on the design set (measured median half-widths 1.30, 1.00 and 0.031 points); candidates are ranked by constraint domination, so an infeasible candidate never outranks a feasible one. A run that finds nothing feasible selects the shipped tuning itself and reports 0.00. An identity check, the shipped tuning evaluated through the same path, returns exactly 0.000 in every run.

\paragraph{One line of the shipped file.} The first row changes nothing but the corner frequency of the generator-speed low-pass filter, from the shipped 1.57\,rad/s to 3.0, with the gain schedule and the damper untouched. On fresh seeds it reduces generator-speed MSE by 23.0\,\% $[22.1, 23.8]$, power MSE by 17.7\,\% and tower-base fatigue by 8.7\,\% $[7.6, 9.8]$, with blade fatigue and energy slightly improved, at a pitch travel of 1.000 times the shipped controller and a duty cycle of 0.048 against 0.0476. Nothing is traded: the shipped corner is a rule of thumb that costs the loop phase, and a faster one buys regulation for free up to about 3.4\,rad/s. On the design screen the same single knob gives $+22.6$ at 3.0\,rad/s and 1.004x travel, $+25.9$ at 3.8\,rad/s (the 3P frequency) and 1.06x, $+27.4$ at 5.0 and 1.18x, and $+28.6$ at 6.5 and 1.57x: a ladder whose price is duty. The agent found the 3.0\,rad/s row in eight simulations and the 6.5\,rad/s row in the same run, and its transcript shows how: it sweeps one quantity at a time with the plant's own frequencies (the tower mode, 3P, the filter ceiling) as landmarks.

\paragraph{The band is a duty ladder.} The second row, the same line at 6.5\,rad/s, buys $+28.4$ of regulation and $+16.3$ of tower fatigue for 1.65x travel. Stage~1, a classical recipe that adds the tower damper at 0.033 and is not an agent arm, buys the last nine points of tower fatigue ($+25.3$) for a further 0.37x of travel and reaches 2.02x; its regulation is within 1.4 points of the agent's 6.5\,rad/s row. The damper is what buys tower fatigue, and it is also where an earlier search went wrong: with its gain capped at 0.004, worth 0.25$^\circ$ of pitch authority, ROSCO's tower damper had looked worth nothing; searched over a box read off the plant it asks about 2$^\circ$ and buys most of stage~1's tower term. At the bottom of the ladder a local-search row holds every term at \emph{less} travel than the shipped controller, 0.974x, buying $+17.0$ of tower fatigue and $+11.4$ of regulation. Figure~\ref{fig:front} shows where these rows sit among everything the campaign evaluated, and Fig.~\ref{fig:overlay} overlays the agent's rows on the shipped controller as levels rather than reductions.

\paragraph{Random search finds nothing.} Over 72 candidates drawn from the same box the random arm never produces a candidate that holds every constraint on the set mean with positive regulation; each of its runs therefore selects the shipped tuning and reports zero. The three searches that use the history do, in 4, 5 and 5 runs of 6.

\paragraph{Every candidate the agent proposed, the plant could run.} Of 144 agent candidates none destabilised the plant, against 13 of 144 for the local search (Fisher two-sided $p = 0.00018$), 9 of 144 for Bayesian optimisation ($p = 0.0034$) and 16 of 72 for random draws ($p < 10^{-5}$); the audit of malformed replies reads zero in all six agent runs, so all 144 were really proposed by the model. The classical arms spend 6 to 9\,\% of their simulation budget on controllers that cannot run; on a 600\,s aeroelastic simulation that is machine time. This is a post hoc observation, not a pre-registered endpoint (§\ref{sec:discussion}), and it concerns the admissibility of the proposals, not the robustness of the delivered tunings, for which no margin or plant-parameter sweep was run.

\paragraph{The design screen ranks correctly.} Of the 21 set-mean selections, 21 remain feasible on both held-out sets; the design-screen regulation of a selection correlates with its held-out value at $+1.00$ (rank $+1.00$), with an optimism of $-0.43$ points on average and $+0.88$ at worst. The 14-episode screen is a reliable ranking of the set mean; what it cannot do, with two episodes per wind, is resolve a per-wind load term to better than about half a point, which is why the per-wind rule is applied as a screen there and as a measurement on 56 episodes (App.~\ref{app:delivery}).

\section{The untuned MPC, scheduled by the agent}
\label{sec:leg2}

The second object is a controller the agent did not design: an offset-free linear-parameter-varying MPC with three states, a 2\,s horizon re-linearised every 0.1\,s from the $C_P$/$C_T$ tables, solved with OSQP~\citep{Stellato2020osqp} in 3.6\,ms, its cost terms normalised to $O(1)$ and a 5\,s output-disturbance estimate on the speed channel making it offset-free~\citep{Liu2024offsetfree} (App.~\ref{app:mpc}). Hand-set, with a constant tower weight, it is a strong regulator and a feasibility failure: Table~\ref{tab:mpc} and Fig.~\ref{fig:perwind} show that its tower-base fatigue at 24\,m/s is $-8.76$ points against the shipped controller on fresh seeds ($-6.27$ held out), while its set-mean tower term, $+15.6$, hides this entirely.

The agent's design space here is the cost structure: a tower weight and a schedule of it on the controller's wind-speed estimate~\citep{Wintermeyer2021weightsched}. From the unscheduled reference, in 24 simulations, the agent found a schedule that ramps the tower weight up above 18\,m/s and brings the 24\,m/s tower term from $-8.76$ to $-0.33$, inside the 1-point tolerance, and 22\,m/s from $+0.55$ to $+3.44$ (held out: $-6.27\to+0.08$ and $-2.46\to+0.24$), at a cost of 1.7 points of regulation. A matched evolution-strategy proposer found the same thing in the same budget; random search found nothing in 48 candidates; and neither proposer beat the hand design of the schedule that a human later produced, so this is a feasibility repair and not a level gain, and at $-0.33$ the repaired term is inside the tolerance, not positive.

\section{The learned layer, and the reward the agent writes for it}
\label{sec:leg3}

The third object is the reward expression of a learned residual layer. Two PPO policies~\citep{Schulman2017ppo}, one per operating region, add a bounded collective-pitch offset to the base controller's command, second-order damped, limited to 0.0744\,rad below rated and 0.05\,rad above; the pitch limits apply to the sum, so with a zero residual the controller \emph{is} the base. The increment is multiplied by a gate on a 10\,s low-pass of the controller's own wind-speed estimate, $g(v)=\mathrm{clip}((v-15)/2,0,1)$, present during training so that selection sees it; the gate is prior art in mechanism~\citep{Kim2026stabilityaware,Abbas2026specialized} and is ours only in its variable. Training uses 150\,s episodes of the seven design winds under a per-wind constrained objective (App.~\ref{app:objective}); every reported number is a 600\,s evaluation on held-out winds. Every 32 training episodes the agent receives the diagnostics, the evaluation, the decision history and the objective, and returns three candidate reward expressions; each is trained for an eight-episode fork and the best continues, with fork episodes charged to the same 300-episode budget (App.~\ref{app:controllers}). Nothing about the loop differs from §\ref{sec:leg1} except what the proposer writes.

Trained on top of the stage-1 tuned baseline of Table~\ref{tab:band}, three seeds, the agent-written reward gives the layer of Table~\ref{tab:gated}: paired against its own base on the same 28 fresh episodes it adds $+5.8$ to $+6.4$ points of power MSE and $+5.9$ to $+6.8$ of speed MSE, every interval clear of zero, with tower fatigue $+0.01$ to $+0.64$ (never negative, one seed unresolved) and blade fatigue $+0.5$ to $+1.5$, at 1.22 to 1.29 times the base's pitch travel; the held-out set gives the same ordering. Under the per-wind rule, against its own base, the layer holds in 3 of 3 seeds on both sets, the narrowest by 0.02 points at 18\,m/s, a count a re-run could flip. Below the gate the layer is exactly the base; above it the gain is in regulation, and Fig.~\ref{fig:perwind} shows where. Three features of the expressions the agent writes recur and none is a coefficient a weight search can reach: the regulation terms are saturated, the fatigue terms are one-sided and bounded, and the tower term's weight is itself a function of the wind label (App.~\ref{app:rewards}). What the proposer is and is not worth here is measured with two matched controls and reported in App.~\ref{app:controls}: on the ungated base the agent's structure separates from a random draw of its own grammar on constraint satisfaction; under the gate it does not, because the gate removes the wind bins where the constraint binds.

\section{How the numbers were read}
\label{sec:discipline}

Three choices of reporting decide the apparent size of every number above, and the paper fixes them rather than leaving them to the reader.

\paragraph{Per wind speed, off the design winds.} A set-mean load constraint is gamed across wind speeds, and this study measured four instances: a learned residual that improved tower fatigue at one wind speed while losing it at another and still scored well; the unscheduled MPC of §\ref{sec:leg2}, $+15.6$ on the set mean and $-8.8$ at 24\,m/s; and, twice, classical tunings of the shipped controller that held the set-mean tower term inside the measurement's resolution while losing 2.7 to 7.6 points of it at a single wind speed (App.~\ref{app:delivery}). In every instance the size of the set-mean margin predicted whether the tuning held per wind. The rule of §\ref{sec:protocol} is the consequence, and it is applied where it can be measured: on the 14-episode design set the per-wind rule rejects the stage-1 tuning at $-1.1$ (blade, 22\,m/s) although the same tuning reads $-0.24$ and $-0.34$ on the two 28-episode sets.

\paragraph{One aggregation, stated.} Every reduction above is the mean of per-episode percentage reductions, which is what the published set uses; a damage-equivalent load aggregated over a site distribution is a different quantity, a power mean of exponent $m$ within and across bins, and Table~\ref{tab:weighting} gives both for the same controllers with IEC Rayleigh weights at three annual means, renormalised over the evaluated above-rated bins. The weighting is not cosmetic. The model-based controller's regulation advantage over the tuned baseline, $+25.6$ points per episode, is $+13.7$ at $V_{\mathrm{ave}}=10$\,m/s and $+1.9$ at 7.5\,m/s under site weighting, while its tower-fatigue disadvantage, $-7.9$ per episode and $-4.5$ equally weighted as a level ratio, becomes an advantage of $+2.8$ to $+6.9$ under the same weights, because the weights favour the bins near rated where the MPC's tower term is largest. These weighted aggregates carry no interval and are reported as the convention's effect, not as a result. The W\"ohler exponent, by contrast, changes magnitudes and not the ordering of any pair of rows (App.~\ref{app:sensitivity}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What transfers.} The search space and the protocol do; the numbers do not. A tuning file the controller can take, a box read off the plant, a constrained rule whose tolerances are the measurement's own intervals, and a per-wind constraint measured off the design winds, are the parts of this study another turbine inherits unchanged. The loop itself is unchanged across three objects whose only common property is that a candidate can be written down and a simulator can judge it.

\paragraph{What the agent costs.} One call per candidate at reasoning effort medium, against a 600\,s aeroelastic simulation of fourteen episodes per candidate; the call is not the expensive part. What the agent saves is the 6 to 9\,\% of candidates the classical searches spend on controllers that cannot run, and the engineering of an acquisition function or a population for each new space.

\paragraph{What this does not show.} Two comparisons were pre-registered before the campaign of §\ref{sec:search} was run, and both are reported in full in App.~\ref{app:prereg} and~\ref{app:delivery}. The primary one, the number of simulations to the first feasible gain, does not separate the agent from Bayesian optimisation ($p = 0.31$, one-sided exact permutation) or from the local search ($p = 0.19$) at six runs per arm; it separates the agent from random draws ($p = 0.048$). And under the rule that ships, a tuning holding both loads at every wind speed off the design winds, the classical arms' selections delivered more often than the agent's: the agent's selection rule, applied to its own candidates, chose the most-regulating row in every run, and the most-regulating rows are exactly the ones that lose the tower-base load at 24\,m/s off the design winds, as Fig.~\ref{fig:front}(b) shows; the agent had itself proposed the two rows of Table~\ref{tab:band} that hold, in the same run, and the 14-episode screen could not tell them from the row it chose. The positive results of this paper do not depend on either comparison; the one proposer-level difference that is large, the admissibility of the agent's candidates, was observed post hoc and is reported as such. The cheapest open question in the study follows from it: a screen with a margin, or design episodes spent at the winds where the constraint binds, would let the rule hand a user the rows the agent already found.

\paragraph{Limits.} One turbine, one turbulence class, simulation only, six runs per arm. The agent's runs repeat each other (two distinct per-wind selections in three), which is a property of a model at fixed effort and not of the search space. No robustness margin or plant-parameter sweep was run on the delivered tunings. The gate, the weight schedule and offset-free MPC are prior art and are claimed only as measured.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
A language-model agent placed in the simulation loop of a wind turbine's pitch controller, with the simulator deciding everything the agent proposes, found in eight simulations that one line of the shipped ROSCO tuning file is worth 23\,\% of generator-speed regulation and 9\,\% of tower-base fatigue at the shipped pitch duty, and a second line worth 28\,\% and 16\,\% for 1.65 times the travel, each holding every load term at every wind speed on 56 episodes it was not designed on, where random search over the same box found nothing in 72. The same loop repaired an untuned model-predictive controller's per-wind feasibility failure in 24 simulations and wrote the reward structure of a learned layer that adds six points of regulation to the tuned baseline without losing tower fatigue. Across 144 candidates it proposed none the plant could not run. The protocol under which this was measured, loads per wind speed off the design winds, paired intervals, duty beside every row, is what makes the claims checkable, and it is the part of this paper that should outlive its numbers.

\section{Data, caveats and the composite that is not reported}
\label{app:objective}
\paragraph{Wind bank and baselines.} The TurbSim fields and the paired shipped-controller rollouts are generated once and never regenerated; every evaluation pairs against the same rollout on the same wind file, and Fig.~\ref{fig:overlay} is the check that it does. After any interruption the artefacts are integrity-checked before use.

\paragraph{The composite selection objective.} The campaigns of §\ref{sec:leg2} and §\ref{sec:leg3} selected and trained on a composite of the four reductions, their mean with each term clipped to $\pm100$ and a penalty of 20 points per percent of energy loss beyond 1\,\%, under a per-wind variant that charges 20 points for every metric falling more than 1 point below the base at any single wind speed. \section{Controllers and training}
\label{app:controllers}
\paragraph{Learned layer.} PPO with $\gamma=0.998$ (a 0.99 discount is myopic with respect to the $\approx3$\,s tower mode and every method fails with it), GAE $\lambda=0.98$~\citep{Schulman2016gae}, $64\times64$ tanh networks, critic targets standardised by a running return-scale estimate~\citep{vanHasselt2016popart}, 300 episodes of 150\,s per run, eight parallel OpenFAST workers. Observations are generator-speed error and its rate, measured pitch, hub wind speed, blade-root out-of-plane moment, tower-top fore-aft acceleration and the base's command. The region is decided by the base controller's own pitch command, above rated once it has exceeded $0.5^\circ$ continuously for 1\,s. The reported checkpoint is the best on the design winds under the per-wind objective; the last checkpoint is frequently degraded and is never used. The load proxies in the reward are the growth of the 10\,s trailing peak-to-peak range of tower acceleration and blade-root moment; the instantaneous moment and its increment both destabilise training.

\paragraph{Supervisor.} The model is \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, in JSON object mode, at reasoning effort \emph{medium} and a 6000-token completion limit; the provider rejects any temperature other than its default. In §\ref{sec:leg1} one call is made per candidate, 24 per run; in §\ref{sec:leg3} five to six calls per run, each returning three candidate reward expressions trained for an eight-episode fork, so a supervised run spends 180 episodes on its main trajectory and 120 in verification. The random-structure control of App.~\ref{app:controls} draws from a fixed grammar of the term shapes the agent itself has used, under the same loop, the same fork length and the same budget.

\section{The search on the shipped controller, in full}
\label{app:search}
\paragraph{Why these five quantities.} An earlier search scaled the shipped gain tables by two scalars. Because ROSCO builds the schedule as $K_p=(2\zeta_{pc}\omega_{pc}+A(\beta))/B(\beta)$ and $K_i=\omega_{pc}^2/B(\beta)$, a scalar on $K_p$ implies a damping ratio that varies 6.8 to 12.8\,\% along the schedule, and no tuning file can produce those tables. Every candidate in this paper is built from $(\omega_{pc},\zeta_{pc})$ through the toolbox's own formula.

\paragraph{The box, every limit measured on the plant.} First tower fore-aft mode 0.3241\,Hz $=2.037$\,rad/s (peak of the tower-base moment spectrum over the 70 above-rated baseline episodes); 3P at rated speed 3.797\,rad/s; 99.99\,\% of the generator-speed signal power below 19.2\,rad/s; the damper's high-passed input reaches 1.076\,m/s on a median episode; shipped pitch travel 0.464\,deg/s mean absolute rate, 4.6\,\% of the 10\,deg/s limit. Hence $\omega_{pc}\in[0.10, 2.04]$\,rad/s, $\zeta_{pc}\in[0.10, 2.00]$, \texttt{F\_LPFCornerFreq} $\in[0.20, 19.2]$\,rad/s, \texttt{FA\_KI} $\in[0, 0.20]$\,rad$\cdot$s/m, \texttt{FA\_HPFCornerFreq} $\in[0.05, 1.20]$\,rad/s, \texttt{FA\_IntSat} fixed at $10^\circ$ and reported whenever it binds. An earlier box capped the filter corner at 4.0\,rad/s and the damper gain at 0.004, and both of the agent's winners under it sat on the corner cap while the damper could move the pitch by $0.25^\circ$; that box, not the plant, set its result.

\paragraph{The rule.} Maximise the generator-speed MSE reduction on the design set subject to tower DEL, blade DEL and energy no worse than shipped, a constraint violated only where its loss exceeds its own 95\,\% paired-bootstrap interval; rank by constraint domination (feasible before infeasible, then by total shortfall, then by the objective). The identity check, the shipped tuning through the same path, reads 0.000 in all 21 runs; the campaign recorded zero infrastructure failures.

\section{What each arm delivers under the rule that ships}
\label{app:delivery}
Each run's per-wind selection, the candidate the rule selects when the load constraints are also required at every wind speed on the design screen, was measured on the 28 held-out and the 28 fresh episodes. A run \emph{delivers} if that candidate holds both loads at every wind speed on both sets. Local search delivered in 4 of 6 runs ($+19.9$ (7.4) points of regulation), Bayesian optimisation in 2 of 6 ($+19.9$ (4.8)), the agent in 0 of 6, random draws in 0 of 3; Fisher two-sided $p = 0.061$ for the agent against the local search (0.030 one-sided in the direction the data went, which is the opposite of the pre-registered one), $p = 0.45$ against Bayesian optimisation. The agent's two distinct selections miss the tolerance by 0.17 and 2.40 points, on the tower-base load at 24\,m/s, worse on fresh seeds than held out each time. Position on the front is the mechanism: every selection that ships sits between $+11.6$ and $+28.7$ points of regulation, every one that fails between $+26.7$ and $+37.7$, and the agent's selections are the highest rows of all (Fig.~\ref{fig:front}b). In the run that produced both rows of Table~\ref{tab:band}, the rule took a third candidate reading $+37.0$ on the design set with a worst single wind of $-0.60$, because two episodes per wind cannot tell $-0.60$ from $-1.20$; off the design winds that candidate reads $-1.17$ at 24\,m/s. The screen, not the search, is what fails, and the two largest-margin deliveries of the whole campaign are rows the set-mean stage never looked at.

\section{Who writes the reward: the two matched controls}
\label{app:controls}
\paragraph{Ungated base, eight seeds.} On the tuned PI base without the gate, under the per-wind rule at 600\,s, runs whose loads hold are 4 of 5 for the agent-written reward, 1 of 8 for a random reward structure drawn from the agent's own grammar with the same fork verification, and 0 of 8 for a fixed, once-tuned reward: Fisher two-sided $p = 0.032$ against the random structures and $p = 0.007$ against the fixed rewards. The two searched arms are indistinguishable in level on the selection objective; what separates them is the constraint.

\paragraph{Gated base, three seeds, rule pre-registered.} On the baseline this paper headlines, under the rule that decides the question (the worst single-wind load term against the run's own base, tolerance 1 point), the agent-written reward holds in 3 of 3 seeds and the random reward structure in 3 of 3, on both held-out sets, Fisher $p = 1.0$; the control holds with more margin (no control seed is negative at any wind speed held out, against the agent arm's worst cell of $-0.98$) and is not behind in level ($+6.1$ to $+7.9$ points of power MSE added against the agent's $+5.8$ to $+6.4$ on fresh seeds, three seeds per arm, a direction and not a result) while spending more actuation. Both arms are $+0.00$ at 12\,m/s by construction and no worse than $-0.09$ at 14\,m/s, which is exactly where the ungated failures were, so the gate removes the bins where the constraint binds and supplies the feasibility the proposer used to supply. Leg 3 therefore credits the searched reward structure, and credits the proposer in the one configuration where the constraint still binds, which is not the headline one.

\section{The model-based controller: design points}
\label{app:mpc}
The three cost terms, on speed error, tower-top velocity and pitch increment, are each normalised to $O(1)$ at their typical magnitude; with the speed error normalised by rated speed instead, the tower term outweighs regulation by four orders of magnitude, the optimiser feathers the rotor, and the best such controller carries no tower term and loses tower fatigue, an artefact of normalisation that reproduces the familiar impression that MPC buys regulation with fatigue. A 5\,s output-disturbance estimate on the speed channel makes the controller offset-free~\citep{Liu2024offsetfree}; with the power and thrust coefficients 5\,\% low in the internal model the nominal controller loses regulation and the offset-free one does not, and it is flat over $\pm15$\,\% coefficient error. The tower weight that is right near rated is wrong at 22--24\,m/s; raising it globally destabilises 12\,m/s before it repairs 24, which is why the repair is a schedule~\citep{Wintermeyer2021weightsched}. The hand design of that schedule, produced after the agent's, ramps the weight from 3 below 18\,m/s to 6 by 22\,m/s and holds every term non-negative per wind speed on the held-out set; neither the agent nor the evolution strategy beat it.

\section{Reward expressions written by the agent}
\label{app:rewards}
The expression kept by the best run on the tuned baseline, in the sandbox's variables (\texttt{region}: the router's region, 1 above rated; \texttt{region\_w}: the wind label; \texttt{d\_wg}: relative generator-speed error; \texttt{p\_ratio}: power over paired base power; \texttt{load\_t}, \texttt{load\_b}: the fatigue proxies; \texttt{act}: actuation):
\begin{footnotesize}\begin{verbatim}
45*tanh(p_ratio-1)*(1-region)
  - region_w*(36*tanh(abs(d_wg)/.004) + 10*tanh(abs(p_ratio-1)))
  - (1+2*region_w)*tanh(max(load_t-1,0)) - (1+region_w)*tanh(max(load_b-1,0))
  - .06*tanh(act)
\end{verbatim}\end{footnotesize}
and on the scheduled MPC:
\begin{footnotesize}\begin{verbatim}
40*tanh(8*(p_ratio-1))*(1-region) - 22*tanh(180*abs(d_wg))*region_w
  - 10*tanh(8*abs(p_ratio-1))*region_w
  - (1+region)*tanh(max(load_t-1,0)) - 1.2*tanh(max(load_b-1,0)) - .2*tanh(act)
\end{verbatim}\end{footnotesize}
The regulation terms are saturated, so a single gust cannot dominate an episode's return; the fatigue terms are one-sided and bounded, charging only the excess of the proxy over its baseline value; and the tower term's weight is a function of the wind label. A search over the coefficients of a fixed reward cannot reach any of the three.

\section{Individual pitch: where the same loop stops winning}
\label{app:ipc}
The same supervisor, given an individual-pitch action channel on top of the collective one, found no reward under which the layer improved blade or tower fatigue on held-out winds without losing regulation; the result is kept because it bounds the claim, and because the objectives under which individual pitch does pay in the recent literature, tower clearance and blade-only fatigue at a stated actuation cost, are not the four-term set measured here. The code paths stay in the released tree, inert.

\section{Budget, compute and availability}
\label{app:compute}
Leg 1: 21 runs of 24 candidates, each candidate 14 episodes of 600\,s (seven winds, two seeds) on seven parallel OpenFAST workers; every set-mean selection and every per-wind selection re-evaluated on 56 further episodes. Leg 2: 24 candidates per run for the agent and the evolution strategy, 48 for random draws, each on the design set. Leg 3, per run: 180 main-trajectory episodes and 120 fork episodes of 150\,s plus 168 evaluation episodes, 19.5\,h of simulated time, matched across the agent and the random-structure arms; the fixed-reward control spends 300 main episodes and 154 evaluation episodes, 18.9\,h. All simulations run OpenFAST~4.2.1 with ROSCO~2.10.5 through a patched ZeroMQ interface at a 10\,ms control step, on one 16-thread AMD Ryzen~7~6800H under WSL2. Evaluations of deterministic controllers are exactly reproducible given the wind fields; training runs are reproducible given the seed.

\paragraph{Code and data availability.} The controllers, the search and training pipeline, the wind-field generation script, every figure script and the per-episode evaluation artefacts behind every table are public (\repourl); each figure script re-derives its numbers from those artefacts and refuses to run if they disagree with the committed tables. OpenFAST~4.2.1 and ROSCO~2.10.5 are third-party open-source software, and the ROSCO patch that exposes the controller state over ZeroMQ is included.
