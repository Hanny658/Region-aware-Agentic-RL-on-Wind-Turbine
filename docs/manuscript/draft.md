# Where should a language-model agent act when it supervises a learning controller? Levers, objectives and verification on a wind-turbine pitch-control benchmark

*Draft v1, 2026-09-13. Every number below is traceable to a run directory or a CSV in `docs/tables/`;
figures are generated by `scripts/dev/manuscript_figures.py`. Citation keys refer to
`references.md`, where each entry carries its verification status; `[Baseline]` is the region-III
RL pitch-control paper whose metric set is adopted here and whose bibliographic details are still
to be supplied.*

## Abstract

Large-language-model (LLM) agents are increasingly used to supervise reinforcement-learning (RL)
controllers — writing reward functions, tuning weights, choosing hyper-parameters. We ask two
questions such systems rarely answer: *at which layer* does the agent add value, and *what does
the verification loop around it guarantee*? We answer them with a controlled study on
collective-pitch control of the NREL 5 MW turbine in OpenFAST, where a region-aware residual PPO
policy is supervised every 30 training episodes by an agent that may act on one of three levers —
the reward's weights, the learner's hyper-parameters, or the reward's expression — under two
objectives: a load-priority fitness F with a speed constraint, and the metric set of a published
RL pitch-control study, J. Each lever is paired with a random-search control, evaluated on two
disjoint held-out wind sets, and judged against tuned fixed defaults and a model-based reference.
Three results follow. (i) Under F the LLM proposer beats random search on reward weights in 7 of 7
seeds, while fork verification adds nothing to a good proposer. (ii) Under J, hyper-parameter
supervision is worth +6 to +8 J on a default whose training collapses, and nothing once the
default is repaired; the repair itself — a normalised value target and a reward gated by the
objective's own subset — is where most of the apparent gain lived. (iii) Only the agent that
rewrites the reward's *shape* reaches the balanced solution in which all four metrics improve
(3 of 5 seeds), which no weight search, weight sweep, hyper-parameter search or combined agent
reaches; it does so whether it is told F or J, and not without the verification loop, and its
effect on the level of J is inside seed noise. A linear-parameter-varying MPC whose cost is
correctly scaled remains the strongest controller on J (≈ 12 on both wind sets) — provided its
aerodynamic model is exact; ±15 % on Cp/Ct removes its lead entirely. We distil the findings into
design rules: put the agent on structural decisions, verify against the true objective rather than
trusting the prompt, one lever per agent, and repair the default before crediting the agent.

## 1. Introduction

Reward design and hyper-parameter tuning are the two chores of applied RL, and LLM agents now
do both: Eureka evolves reward code against a task score [Ma2024], Language-to-Rewards and
Text2Reward map instructions to reward parameters or dense reward programs [Yu2023, Xie2024],
DrEureka adds domain-randomisation design [Ma2024b], and agentic reward designers close the loop
with semantic feedback on trajectories [Lee2026]. What these systems share is an outer loop:
propose, train briefly, score, keep the best. What they leave open is a pair of questions that
decide whether the agent is doing useful work or merely expensive search:

1. **Which layer?** The same loop can be pointed at numeric knobs (reward weights, learning
   rates) or at structure (which terms a reward has, what shape they take). A proposer that
   understands the problem should matter more where the search space is semantic and less where a
   log-uniform perturbation already covers it. Nobody has measured that on one problem with
   matched controls.
2. **What does verification guarantee?** If the proposals are scored on the true objective, the
   prompt's description of the objective may not matter at all; conversely, an unverified proposal
   may be no better than the default.

Wind-turbine collective-pitch control above rated wind is a good place to ask, because the
objective is genuinely multi-criteria (speed regulation, power quality, tower and blade fatigue),
because strong model-based references exist [Bossanyi2000, Soliman2011, Koerber2013,
Schlipf2013, Evans2015], and because a recent RL study on the IEA 15 MW turbine defines a
metric set and reports an MPC comparison [Baseline] that we can adopt and test.

**Contributions.**
(a) A controlled protocol for agentic supervision — three levers × two objectives, random-search
controls for every numeric lever, seed pairing, two disjoint held-out wind sets, exact
permutation tests, and tuned fixed defaults — applied to a residual PPO pitch controller on the
NREL 5 MW turbine.
(b) The finding that the objective decides which lever "works": the proposer matters on reward
weights under a load-priority objective; nothing numeric matters under the metric-set objective
once the default is sound; the reward's shape is the one lever that changes what the solution is
made of.
(c) Two mechanism results that explain the apparent gains of agentic supervision: a collapsed
critic (value target four orders of magnitude beyond the network's reach) and a rollback loop
caused by a reward gated on a different subset than the objective — both repaired without any
agent, and both worth more than the agent.
(d) A corrected model-based reference: the same three-state LPV-MPC that "loses tower fatigue"
under a naively scaled cost is, once the cost terms are scaled to O(1), positive on all four
metrics and ahead of every RL arm — with an exact aerodynamic model, and only then.

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 Plant and baseline controller

NREL 5 MW onshore turbine [Jonkman2009] in OpenFAST 4.2.1 [OpenFAST], controlled by ROSCO 2.10.5
[Abbas2022] with a patched ZeroMQ interface exposing 22 measurement channels at the 10 ms control
step. The ROSCO configuration is the gain-scheduled PI (GSPI) with peak shaving on and every other
load-mitigation feature off (no tower damper, no floating feedback, no IPC), constant-torque
above rated; this is the pairing reference for every metric. Wind: TurbSim fields at 8, 12.5 and
15 m/s mean hub wind, TI 8 %, 150 s episodes of which 130 s are scored; seeds S1–S2 are the
supervisor's (training-side) evaluation winds, S3–S6 and S7–S10 are two disjoint held-out sets.
Stress classes (TI 14 %, TI 22 % at 15 m/s, 18 m/s) are used for the reference controller only.
Fatigue is the damage-equivalent load from rainflow counting [fatpack, IEC61400-1].

### 2.2 Residual RL controller

Two PPO [Schulman2017] agents — one per operating region, selected by an oracle rule on ROSCO's
native pitch command — add a bounded collective-pitch offset (|Δβ| ≤ 0.05 rad, second-order
damped) to the GSPI command. Observations: generator-speed error and its rate, measured pitch,
hub wind speed, blade-root out-of-plane moment, tower-top fore-aft acceleration. Discount γ = 0.998, GAE λ = 0.98
[Schulman2016], 64×64 tanh networks, 300 training episodes per run. The per-step reward is
region-conditional: a power term in Region 2, a speed-error term in Region 3, two fatigue proxies
(the growth of the 10 s peak-to-peak range of tower acceleration and of blade-root moment), and an
actuation penalty; its weights are the six *knobs* a supervisor may change. Reward **v1** is the
historical form (exponential speed term, one load proxy); **v2** uses a quadratic speed term and
both proxies; **v3** is v2 with the speed term gated by the wind label (hub wind above rated)
instead of the router's region — the subset on which the objective's regulation metrics are
scored (§3.2). The critic's regression target is standardised by a running estimate of the return
scale, in the spirit of Pop-Art [vanHasselt2016] (§3.2 shows why).

### 2.3 The supervision loop and its levers

Every 30 episodes the policy is evaluated on S1–S2 and a supervisor may act. The agent (an
OpenAI-compatible reasoning model, JSON mode, ~5 calls per run) receives the training diagnostics,
the evaluation, the decision history with outcomes, and the objective's definition; it returns
K = 3 candidates. Each candidate is trained for a 30-episode fork from the same checkpoint, the
forks are evaluated, and the best fork continues (*fork verification*). A guardrail rolls the run
back to its best checkpoint when the objective drops by more than 5 points (or, under J, when
energy loss exceeds 1 %). The three levers:

- **weights** — the six reward/bound knobs (log-uniform random search as control);
- **hyper-parameters** — actor/critic learning rates, GAE λ, clip range, entropy, policy std
  (random search as control);
- **reward code** — a sandboxed Python expression over (region, wind label, speed error, power
  ratio, both load proxies, actuation) that replaces the parametric reward, in the manner of
  Eureka [Ma2024].

Fixed-knob training under the same guardrail is the *guard* baseline; a single-proposal agent
without forks, and an agent asked once and applied blind, are the loop ablations.

### 2.4 Objectives

**F** (load priority): tower-DEL reduction minus 20 points per % of energy loss beyond 1 % and per
% of Region-3 speed-std ratio above the GSPI's — speed regulation as a constraint, reported in
tiers (strict / tolerance / degraded).
**J** (the metric set of [Baseline]): the mean of the four % reductions relative to paired GSPI —
power MSE and generator-speed MSE on wind-labelled Region-3 steps, tower-base and blade-root DEL —
each clipped per episode to ±100, minus 20 per % energy loss beyond 1 %; no tiers. Under J every
selection (fork winner, best checkpoint, rollback) uses J. All reported numbers are held-out
evaluations of the best checkpoint by the supervisor-side objective.

### 2.5 Statistics

Seeds are paired across arms (same wind realisations, same seed index); differences are reported
as mean, wins/n, the exact two-sided sign-flip permutation p (resolution floor 2/2ⁿ: 0.0156 at
n = 7, 0.0625 at n = 5, 0.25 at n = 3) and a paired t-test. n = 3 arms are proofs of concept and
are labelled as such.

### 2.6 The model-based reference

A three-state LPV-MPC (rotor, first tower fore-aft mode) linearised every 0.1 s at the operating
point from the Cp/Ct tables, N = 20, OSQP [Stellato2020] (3.6 ms per solve), pitch within the
live peak-shaving floor and rate limit, wind held at ROSCO's low-passed estimate (no preview,
unlike [Schlipf2013, Koerber2013]), torque native. Cost: speed error, tower-top velocity, pitch
increment. Weights are selected on S1–S2 by J — the same rule as the RL arms — and the controller
is evaluated both with full pitch authority and through the RL's own bounded, damped residual
channel.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Objective F: the proposer matters on reward weights (Fig. 1a)

| arm (F, best checkpoint) | n | F on S3–S6 | F on S7–S10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed weights (guard) | 5 | 13.93 ± 2.29 | 12.54 ± 1.44 |
| LLM weights, fork-verified | 7 | 17.89 ± 2.02 | 15.84 ± 1.63 |
| random weights, fork-verified | 7 | 12.36 ± 5.30 | 10.87 ± 4.55 |
| LLM weights, single proposal, no forks | 5 | 17.92 ± 1.57 | 15.75 ± 1.78 |

The LLM proposer beats the same fork search with random candidates in 7 of 7 seeds on both wind
sets (+5.5 / +5.0, exact p = 0.0156), beats fixed weights in 5 of 5 (+3.5 / +2.7, p = 0.0625),
and the single-proposal agent is indistinguishable from the verified one (−0.5 / −0.5, 3/5,
p = 0.94 / 0.75). On a six-dimensional weight space with semantic structure (which load term to
move, when to release the action bound), the proposer carries the value and verification only
protects against the occasional bad move. All F-era arms share the original, unnormalised critic.

### 3.2 Objective J, numeric levers: the objective and the default decide (Fig. 1b, Fig. 3)

**Repairing the default without an agent.** Two defects of the learner were found on the way to
J and fixed for every arm. First, the Region-3 critic was a constant: with a 10 ms step and
γ = 0.998 the discounted return is O(10⁴) while the network starts at gain 0.1 under gradient
clipping, so the second layer saturates at 100 % and the value has zero variance (Fig. 3a);
standardising the target restores it (0 % saturation, corr(V, −|δω|) = +0.44) and is worth
+4.3 F on held-out winds by itself. Second, under J with the v2 reward every fixed-weight run
found an early optimum and then rolled back at every later decision, because the policy kept
re-entering a state that regulates worse at 12.5 m/s — the reward's speed term was gated on the
router's region, the objective's speed metric on the wind label, and on the transition steps the
policy was rewarded for load reduction that J scored as regulation loss (Fig. 3b). Gating the
speed term on the wind label (reward v3) removes the loop.

**Hyper-parameters.** On the untuned v2 default the hyper-parameter agent is worth +5.7 / +8.0 J
and random hyper-parameter search +5.8 / +7.5 (3/3 seeds each); the two are indistinguishable
(−0.1 / +0.6, p ≈ 0.9). Both work the same way: they lengthen the credit window (GAE λ 0.98 →
0.995) and slow the actor, which lets the learner escape the rollback loop. On the repaired,
J-tuned default the same arms are worth −0.5 / +0.3 (LLM) and −0.8 / −0.9 (random): the lever's
entire value was the repair of a broken default, and the repair is cheaper without an agent.

**Weights under J.** The fork search over the six weights (reward v3) raises J by +2.1 / +1.9 over
the untuned v3 guard but never keeps a candidate with a heavier tower weight: tower fatigue
responds over tens of episodes, regulation within one fork, so a 30-episode verification horizon
is structurally myopic for that knob (Fig. 3c). A sweep of the tower weight confirms the region is
not reachable on that axis at all — λ_T = 3 / 10 / 30 give J 1.4 / −2.9 / (no learning) against
5.0 at λ_T = 1 — because the range-increment proxy scaled up is not the MPC's tower-velocity
cost; it is load noise.

Three observations.

1. **The level of J does not move.** Every RL arm on the tuned default sits between 4.7 and 8.6
   on S3–S6; the reward-code agent's paired difference to the guard is +0.4 / +0.5 (3/5 seeds,
   exact p = 0.75 / 0.69). The +2.5 of its first three seeds became +0.4 at five: the agent's
   effect on the *level* of the objective is inside seed noise.
2. **The composition of J does.** Every fixed-reward and hyper-parameter arm buys regulation with
   tower fatigue (−13 to −18 %): under J's 2 : 1 pricing of regulation that is net positive, but
   it is the opposite of what a residual load controller is for. The reward-code agent is the
   only RL arm whose arm-mean tower DEL is positive (+1.5 %) and the only one with seeds on which
   all four metrics improve (Fig. 2). All of its balanced seeds share one structure: a *saturated*
   speed term `tanh(|δω|/0.005)` on the wind-labelled subset — steep near zero where the
   quadratic is flat, bounded where it explodes — and centred, bounded load terms of the form
   `tanh(load − 1)` that penalise only above-baseline load and cannot be swamped by regulation.
   That is a change of shape, not of weight, and §3.2 showed the weight axis cannot reach it.
3. **The prior needs the loop; the loop does not need the prompt.** Told F — a load-priority
   objective with speed as a constraint — the same agent writes the same shapes and, with J doing
   the selection, lands on the balanced solution as often (2/3) with a third of the variance
   (8.6 / 9.9); the fork records show it choosing between candidates whose F and J disagreed
   (F 20.7 vs J −0.1 on one), and J won each time because J selects. Asked once and applied
   blind, the same agent is the guard (4.7 / 6.1, 0/3 all-positive): its first expressions are
   of the right family, but without verification two of three seeds keep the guard's tower
   trade. Combining the reward lever with the hyper-parameter lever in one agent is worse than
   the reward lever alone (6.3 / 8.1, 0/3): the combined agent raised learning rates and wrote
   quadratic terms with linear load penalties — one question per agent is the better design.

### 3.4 The reference: a model-based ceiling, and what it is conditional on (Fig. 4)

The published comparison and our own first implementation agree that MPC "wins regulation and
loses tower fatigue" (−17.6 % tower DEL here). Both are an artefact of cost scaling: with the
speed error normalised by rated speed, the speed term is O(10⁻⁵) at a typical error of 0.5 % while
a tower-velocity term (ẋ / 0.2 m s⁻¹)² is O(1), so any tower weight above zero out-weighs
regulation by four orders of magnitude and the optimum feathers the rotor. Scaling the three
terms to O(1) at their typical values (speed error / 0.005, pitch increment / 0.002 rad) lets the
tower weight act; the J-selected point (qt = 3) is positive on every metric on both wind sets,
12.4 / 14.1 with full authority and **12.0 / 11.6 through the RL's own ±2.9° damped residual
channel** — authority explains at most 2.5 J of its lead — and it is F-strict (15.8 / 18.3). The
best RL seeds of Table 1 come within one to two points of it (10.3 / 10.6, 9.1 / 10.3); the arm means
do not.

The lead is conditional on the model. Perturbing only the controller's model (plant unchanged):
tower frequency ±10 % and modal mass ±20 % cost 0–5 J, off-design turbulence (TI 14 %, 22 %)
none, but ±15 % on Cp/Ct takes the MPC to −0.4 (regulation lost) and −27.5 (over-estimated
aerodynamic torque, regulation collapses while the tower gain survives). The reference row in
Table 1 is therefore "LPV-MPC with an exact aerodynamic model"; a model-free residual policy
trained on the plant has no such exposure, and a model identified from data would sit somewhere
between the two rows.

## 4. Discussion: design rules for agentic supervision

1. **Put the agent on structural decisions; give numeric ones to random search.** Under F the
   proposer won on reward weights because the space was semantic; under J it tied random search
   on hyper-parameters and no numeric lever survived a sound default. The one lever with a
   qualitative effect was the reward's shape.
2. **Verification against the true objective is the guarantee; the prompt is not.** The reward
   agent told the wrong objective produced the right solution because J selected its forks; the
   agent asked once without verification produced the default. Score every proposal on the
   objective the paper reports.
3. **One lever per agent.** The combined agent under-performed each of its parts.
4. **Repair the default before crediting the agent.** The critic's scale, the reward's gating and
   the MPC's cost scaling were each worth more than any agent in this study; two of the three
   "agentic gains" we measured first were repairs of a default that should not have been broken.
5. **Report composition and reliability, not only means.** The agent's real contribution — a
   solution class no other lever reaches, in 3 of 5 seeds — is invisible in a table of mean J.

## 5. Limitations

Seed budgets are 5 for the core arms and 3 for the ablations; the n = 3 differences are labelled
as proofs of concept and none of the agentic J-differences is significant. One plant, one
turbulence intensity for training, two held-out sets of four realisations. The MPC has no preview,
a crude tower-state estimator and a two-second horizon; longer horizons were worse with this
linearisation, which is a property of the model, not of MPC. The residual policy observes the
simulator's hub wind speed where the MPC uses ROSCO's estimate. The agent is one reasoning model
with one prompt family; the F-era arms were trained with the unnormalised critic. An individual-
pitch extension of the same supervisor was attempted and did not win on any of its levers; the
boundary of the present claims is collective pitch.
